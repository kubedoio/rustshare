# RustShare Governance

This document describes how the RustShare project is governed.

## Project Governance

RustShare is currently governed by a small core team operating under a BDFL-style (Benevolent Dictator for Life) model. This is an early-stage open-source project, and the governance structure will evolve as the community grows. The core team is responsible for maintaining project health, setting technical direction, and ensuring the codebase remains high quality and secure.

## Roles

### Maintainers

Maintainers have merge rights, triage issues, and cut releases. They are responsible for reviewing pull requests, enforcing code quality, and making day-to-day project decisions. Currently, maintainers are the core team members who have been involved since the project's inception.

### Contributors

Contributors are anyone who submits issues, pull requests, documentation improvements, or feedback. Contributions of any size are valued, and contributors are the lifeblood of the project's growth.

### Users

Users are anyone deploying or using RustShare in any capacity. User feedback, bug reports, and feature requests are essential inputs that shape the project's direction.

## Decision Making

- **Day-to-day decisions:** Maintainer discretion. Minor changes, bug fixes, documentation updates, and routine maintenance can be approved and merged by any maintainer.
- **Technical direction:** Discussed openly in issues and pull requests. Decisions are made by maintainers after considering community input.
- **Breaking changes:** Require maintainer consensus. Any change that modifies public APIs, database schemas, or deployment behavior must be agreed upon by the core maintainer team.
- **Governance changes:** Require maintainer consensus. Changes to this governance document or project-wide policies must be approved by all core maintainers.

## Becoming a Maintainer

The project may invite regular contributors to become maintainers based on sustained contribution, demonstrated good judgment, and alignment with project values. There is no fixed timeline or checklist for this path. Contributors who consistently produce high-quality work, engage constructively in discussions, and show an understanding of the project's goals may be invited to join the maintainer team.

## Code of Conduct

All participants in the RustShare community are expected to abide by our [Code of Conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).

## Evolution

This governance model is intentionally lightweight and will evolve as the project grows. As the contributor base expands and the project matures, we expect to formalize governance structures, potentially introduce elected roles, and adopt more distributed decision-making processes. Changes to this document will be made transparently and with community input.
